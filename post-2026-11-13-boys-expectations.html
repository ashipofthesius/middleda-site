Expect something from him

November 13, 2026

A boy does a job halfway and then looks around to see if anyone noticed.

He's not checking whether he got away with it. He's checking whether anybody cares enough to make him do it again. The boy is tired, the grown-up is tired, and the thing got done, more or less. But he's watching what happens next, and whatever happens next is what charts the trajectory of his life.

A boy needs grown men and women who expect something from him. A coach who won't put him in until he runs the drill right, a teacher who hands the paper back and says this isn't your best and we both know it, an uncle who makes him finish the fence line before supper, and a daddy who says, "No. You can do better than that," and then stands there while he does. None of that feels like love to a thirteen-year-old. It is, though, and he'll know it by the time he's thirty.

Expectations are how a boy learns that his best matters. If nobody expects much from him, he starts living down to it. No one ever fails to live up to low expectations. But if somebody expects his best, he'll figure out he's capable of more than he thinks he is.

You don't have to be his father to be that person. A coach. A Sunday school teacher. A mentor. Or just anyone who believes hard work bears good fruit. Because a boy who learns that the world will accept a 'just ok' will grow into a man that believes that 'just ok' is all he can do.
