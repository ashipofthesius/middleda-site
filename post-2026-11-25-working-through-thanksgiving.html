Working through Thanksgiving

November 25, 2026

On Thursday most of us will sit down to a big meal with the people we love. A good number of folks in this circuit won't, or they'll do it on somebody else's schedule, because the work they do doesn't stop for a holiday.

The dispatcher is at the console with a headset on all day, answering whatever comes in. It might be a kitchen fire, or it might be a family argument that went too far after the dishes were put up. The dispatcher keeps the caller talking, gets help on the way, and picks up the next line, most of the time without ever finding out how the last one turned out.

The deputy is out on the road answering those calls. That means working the wreck on the way to somebody's mama's house, and it means walking up to a house where the holiday went bad without knowing what's on the other side of the door.

The jailer is inside the jail, where Thanksgiving is a workday like any other. Everybody in there still has to be fed and counted and kept safe, and the jailer has to stay safe doing it. Most folks never think about that building at all, holiday or not, and the jailer is there anyway.

The ER nurse is on a long shift with the regular load plus the kitchen burns and the chest pains, and whatever the ambulance and the deputies bring through the doors. When the news is bad, the nurse is often the one standing with the family.

Their families pay part of the cost. Dinner gets moved up to Wednesday night or pushed back to Saturday. A plate gets wrapped in foil and left in the oven. The kids eat without one of their parents at the table, and somebody comes home late, eats standing at the kitchen counter, and goes to bed so they can be back the next morning.

This Thanksgiving I'm grateful for every dispatcher, deputy, jailer, and ER nurse working through it, and for the families who make room at the table around their shift.
