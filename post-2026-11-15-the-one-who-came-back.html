The one who came back

November 15, 2026

Ten men with leprosy met Jesus at the edge of a village. Luke says they "stood afar off," and from way back there they hollered the only prayer they had: "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us."

Most of us have prayed that prayer at some point, maybe not in those words but at that volume. It's the one you pray in the truck in the hospital parking lot before you go in to hear what the doctor has to say.

Jesus didn't walk over and touch them. He told them to go show themselves to the priests, and "it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed." They got healed on the road, while they were doing what He told them to do.

Then one of them, "when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God." He was every bit as loud thanking God as he'd been begging Him.

The other nine weren't wicked men. They were doing exactly what they'd been told, headed to the priests and then home to whatever was waiting on them, and I expect they were as glad as anybody's ever been. They just never turned around.

Luke adds one more detail almost like an afterthought: "and he was a Samaritan." The one who came back was the outsider. Jesus called him "this stranger."

And Jesus asked, "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?" He kept count, and He noticed who didn't come back.

It's easy to be one of the nine. When the prayer finally gets answered, we're so relieved we go right back to living. The doctor walks in with good news, and we've called everybody in our phone before it crosses our mind to thank the One we were begging out in that parking lot.

All ten were cleansed. Only one got to hear Jesus say, "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole."

If God answered something you begged Him for, today's a good day to turn back and tell Him thank you.
