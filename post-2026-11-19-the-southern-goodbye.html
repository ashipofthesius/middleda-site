Why leaving a Southern get-together takes forty-five minutes

November 19, 2026

Leaving a get-together in the South takes about forty-five minutes, and that’s if nobody needs to talk for ‘just a minute’. It ain’t one thing. By my count, it’s four separate things, and each of them has its own process before you can even think of skedaddling.

First, you say “well, we better get on the road,” Then someone asks about your family, because they haven’t seen them in a while. So, you settle back in for that.

Next, you stand back up. Somebody starts fixing you a plate to take home and wraps it in foil, and somebody else hands you a Cool Whip tub that maybe has butter beans. Maybe has soup. You don’t know, but who doesn’t like surprises.

Third, you make it to the door. You’ve got your keys in one hand and your cool whip mystery container, and then they gotta walk you to the car. Except they don’t really want to go to the car. And it don’t really seem like they want you to get to the car neither. Because there’s just one more thing. But before they can get to the one more thing there’s all the build up.

Finally, you actually make it to the car. You’re seated. In the car. The engine is on, and then they lean in the window. And that’s when you find out how dangerous driving home is. Watch out for deer. Watch out for crazy people on the road. Remember you don’t see so good at night. Call when you get there. Or text. Tell everyone they said hello.

Then you back out slow while the whole house stands in the yard waving. I hear yankees just say ‘bye’ and leave. Bless their hearts.
