Today is Veterans Day.

Every person we honor today started the same way. They raised their right hand and swore to support and defend the Constitution of the United States against all enemies, foreign and domestic. Most of them were eighteen or nineteen when they said it. Some had never been farther from home than Savannah. Then they went where they were sent.

Where they were sent was not up to them. A base in Germany, a ship in the Pacific, a jungle, a desert, a mountain pass in a country most of us couldn't find on a map. They stood guard in the cold. They fixed the engines, carried the radio, flew the plane, drove the truck, loaded the gun, patched up the wounded, and buried their friends. They ate what they were handed and slept where they could. They missed Christmas and birthdays and the births of their own children. Some came home with wounds you can see. More came home with wounds you can't.

They did all that so the rest of us could go to work, go to church, vote, argue with each other on Facebook, and raise our kids without once having to think about who was standing watch. That's the deal. They stood watch, and we got to forget they were there.

Most of them won't tell you about it. You find out when the cap says Vietnam or Korea or Iraq, or when a grandson posts an old picture of a skinny young man in uniform who turns out to be the fellow who fixes your tractor. Ask him about it. Then listen.

To every veteran in Emanuel, Candler, Jefferson, Toombs and Washington counties, and everywhere else: thank you for your service. The rest of us are free because you stood watch, and we know it.
